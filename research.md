## Scoring key
Criteria 1-5, each 1-5: (1) is a short funny line answering a prompt; (2) funny — real laugh with a twist/deadpan turn; (3) original — fresh angle, not the first excuse anyone thinks of; (4) answers the prompt — line fits the situation it excuses and the joke comes from that fit; (5) tight — short, punchy, every word working, punch last.

Why #8 beats #6 on criteria 2-4: the noir alibi turn earns the sharper laugh (2), reframing a fridge's own lamp as evidence-tampering is a fresher angle than the well-worn "final document" gag (3), and the joke is inseparable from fridge behaviour — the light really does go out, the food really does go missing — instead of generic office cynicism worn as a printer costume (4).

## Judge-seat critique of the top three

- #8: A judge sees one concrete appliance action (light out) fused with a crime-novel excuse; the turn lands on the last three words and needs no explanation. Risk to check: keep the word "alibi" as the final beat and add nothing after it.
- #6: Relatable, clean, and the printer voice is weary in a funny way; a judge may mark it down on originality because "final" jokes are a well-trodden lane and "300 copies" is a familiar exaggeration.
- #4: The "ex = familiar" reframe gets a wince-laugh and the legalistic "broad instruction" voice is distinct; a judge may find the step from a music command to a phone call slightly too convenient, which weakens criterion 4.
